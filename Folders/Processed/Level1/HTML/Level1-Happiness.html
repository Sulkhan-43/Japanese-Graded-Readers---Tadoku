みなさんは、「しあわせ」が đại

Minasan wa, "shiawase" ga daisuki desu yo ne?

Everyone loves happiness, don't you?

Vocabulary
  • しあわせ (shiawase) — happiness; good fortune
  • 大好きだいすき (daisuki) — to like very much; to love
Grammar Notes
  • 〜ですよね — Adds a friendly tag seeking agreement, like “right?” after a statement.
Page 42 / 13
では、しあわせは、どんな人ひとのところに来くるのでしょうか。

Dewa, shiawase wa, donna hito no tokoro ni kuru no deshō ka?

Then, what kind of person does happiness come to?

Vocabulary
  • 来くる (kuru) — to come
Grammar Notes
  • どんな N — Use donna before a noun to ask what kind of something it is.
  • 〜のでしょうか — A polite wondering question, often meaning “I wonder...” or “I wonder whether...”.
  • N に 来くる — Means to come to a place; the destination is marked by ni.

あるところに、しあわせの神様かみさまがいました。

Aru tokoro ni, shiawase no kamisama ga imashita.

Once upon a time, there was a god of happiness.

これから、みなさんの家いえに行いきますよ！

Kore kara, minasan no ie ni ikimasu yo!

From now on, I'm going to everyone's house!

あ、でもちょっと待まって…

A, demo chotto matte…

Ah, but wait a moment…

Vocabulary
  • 神様かみさま (kamisama) — god; deity
  • 家いえ (ie) — house; home
  • 行いく (iku) — to go
  • 待まつ (matsu) — to wait
Grammar Notes
  • N に N がいました — Use ni plus ga imashita to say an animate being was at a place; imashita is the polite past form of imasu.
Page 64 / 13
しあわせの神様かみさまは古ふるくて汚きたない服ふくを着きました。

Shiawase no kamisama wa furukute kitanai fuku o kimashita.

The god of happiness wore old, dirty clothes.

お金かねのない、貧乏びんぼうな人ひとの服ふくです。

Okane no nai, binbō na hito no fuku desu.

They are the clothes of a poor person with no money.

Vocabulary
  • 古ふるい (furui) — old
  • 汚きたない (kitanai) — dirty
  • 服ふく (fuku) — clothes
  • 着きる (kiru) — to wear clothing
  • お金かね (okane) — money
  • 貧乏びんぼう (binbō) — poverty; being poor
Grammar Notes
  • i-adjective くて — Connects adjectives by changing the final i to ku and adding te.
  • N のない — Means “without N” or “having no N,” and describes the following noun.

「ああ、貧乏びんぼうか。貧乏びんぼうはすきじゃない」

"Aa, binbō ka. Binbō wa suki ja nai."

“Oh, poverty, huh? I don't like poverty.”

家いえの人ひとは、戸とをピシャンと閉しめました。

Ie no hito wa, to o pishan to shimemashita.

The person in the house slammed the door shut.

Vocabulary
  • 戸と (to) — door, especially a sliding door
  • ピシャン (pishan) — sound of a door slamming shut
  • 閉しめる (shimeru) — to close; to shut
Grammar Notes
  • N じゃない — A casual way to say that something is not N.
  • onomatopoeia と V — An onomatopoeic word followed by to can describe the sound or manner of an action.

家いえの人ひとは、しあわせの神様かみさまに、おにぎりをあげました。

Ie no hito wa, shiawase no kamisama ni, onigiri o agemashita.

The person in the house gave a rice ball to the god of happiness.

Vocabulary
  • おにぎり (onigiri) — rice ball
  • あげる (ageru) — to give
Grammar Notes
  • N に N をあげる — Means to give one thing to someone; the recipient takes ni and the thing given takes o.

Shiawase no kamisama wa, ureshikatta desu.

The god of happiness was happy.

Vocabulary

Grammar Notes
  • i-adjective -katta desu — Forms the polite past of an i-adjective by replacing its final i with katta before desu.

Shiawase no kamisama wa, kono ie ni shiawase o okimashita.
