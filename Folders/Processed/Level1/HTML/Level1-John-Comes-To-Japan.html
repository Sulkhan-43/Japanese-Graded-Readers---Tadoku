JAPANESE GRADED READER · LEVEL 1

John Comes to Japan

ジョンさん日本にほんへ
10 study passagesJapanese · Romaji · English

Repeated vocabulary and grammar explanations are intentionally omitted after their first useful explanation.

Story summary

John came to Japan to study Japanese. He is always making mistakes. What kinds of mistakes and amusing experiences will he have in Japan for the first time?

“Oh!”

Vocabulary
  • 日本にほん (Nihon) — Japan
  • 飛行機ひこうき (hikōki) — airplane
  • 勉強べんきょう (benkyō) — study
  • 鞄かばん (kaban) — bag
  • 漫画まんが (manga) — manga; comic
  • 隣となり (tonari) — next to; neighboring
  • 同おなじ (onaji) — same
Grammar Notes
  • N kara — Kara marks a starting point in time or a source, such as when something begins or where something is taken from.
  • N de — De marks the place where an action happens, or the means used to do it.
  • N mo — Mo means “also” or “too,” adding another person or thing to the statement.

Kabuki starts at four.

“‘Kyū’ no jūgo, jūroku... A, koko desu yo.”

“Section nine, seats fifteen and sixteen... Oh, here they are.”

二人ふたりは席せきに座すわりました。

Futari wa seki ni suwarimashita.

The two of them sat down.

Vocabulary
  • 席せき (seki) — seat
  • 飲のむ (nomu) — to drink
  • ロビー (robī) — lobby
Grammar Notes
  • N kara — Kara marks a starting point in time or a source, such as when something begins or where something is taken from.
  • N de — De marks the place where an action happens, or the means used to do it.
  • N desu ka — Desu ka forms a polite question, often asking whether something is so or requesting information.
  • N desu kara — Desu kara gives a reason: “because it is...” or “since it is...”.
  • V-mashō ka — Mashō ka makes a polite suggestion, such as “Shall we...?”
  • N yo — Yo adds emphasis when giving information or pointing something out to the listener.

“Huh?”

「え？」

“E?”

“Huh?”

Vocabulary
  • おじいさん (ojiisan) — old man; grandfather
  • おばあさん (obaasan) — old woman; grandmother
Grammar Notes
  • Place ni Person ga imasu — This pattern says that a person or other living thing is present at a place.
  • N desu kedo — Kedo means “but”; here it softens a statement that trails off and invites a response.
  • N mo — Mo means “also” or “too,” adding another person or thing to the statement.
  • N yo — Yo adds emphasis when giving information or pointing something out to the listener.
