Story page 11 / 12
早はやく行いきたい！

Hayaku ikitai!

I want to go soon!

わくわく

Wakuwaku!

I'm excited!

柴犬しばいぬのディナちゃんはロシア、サンクトペテルブルグの犬いぬです。

Shiba inu no Dina-chan wa Roshia, Sankuto Peteruburugu no inu desu.

Dina-chan the Shiba Inu is a dog from Saint Petersburg, Russia.

さあ、日本にほんへ行いきましょう。

Sā, Nihon e ikimashō.

Come on, let's go to Japan.

Vocabulary
  • 柴犬しばいぬ (shiba inu) — Shiba Inu; a Japanese dog breed
  • 京都きょうと (Kyōto) — Kyoto
  • 行いく (iku) — to go
  • ロシア (Roshia) — Russia
  • サンクトペテルブルグ (Sankuto Peteruburugu) — Saint Petersburg
Grammar Notes
  • V-stem + たい — Attach tai to a verb stem to say that someone wants to do something.
  • V-stem + ましょう — Attach mashō to a verb stem to make a polite suggestion or invitation, meaning “let's.”
  • Nへ/に + 行いく — Use e or ni after a destination before iku, meaning to go to that place.

Ryokan no heya e hairimasu.

Totemo tsukarete imasu.

でも、散歩さんぽに行いきます。

Demo, sanpo ni ikimasu.

But she goes for a walk.

おなかすいたー

Onaka suitā!

I'm so hungry!

ごはん食たべたい

Gohan tabetai.

I want to eat.

Vocabulary
  • 散歩さんぽ (sanpo) — walk; stroll
  • 食たべる (taberu) — to eat
Grammar Notes
  • V-て + いる — This form can describe an ongoing action or a state that continues; here it describes being tired.

Kuraku narimashita.

鴨川かもがわを歩あるきました。

Kamogawa o arukimashita.

She walked along the Kamo River.

ロシアに帰かえりたくない。

Roshia ni kaeritakunai.

I don't want to go back to Russia.

ずっとここにいたい。

Zutto koko ni itai.

I want to stay here forever.

Vocabulary
  • 暗くらい (kurai) — dark
  • 鴨川かもがわ (Kamogawa) — Kamo River in Kyoto
  • 帰かえる (kaeru) — to return; to go back
Grammar Notes
  • i-adjective + くなる — Change an i-adjective ending to ku before naru to describe becoming that way.
  • V-stem + たくない — This negative form expresses not wanting to do something.

Grammar Notes
  • Nから 来くる — Kara marks where someone comes from; kuru means to come.
  • plain past + の？ — Adding no to a plain-form question can ask for confirmation or an explanation.
