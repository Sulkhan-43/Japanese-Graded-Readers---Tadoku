Story page 11 / 20
森もりの中なかに、うさぎの家族かぞくがいます。

Mori no naka ni, usagi no kazoku ga imasu.

There is a rabbit family in the forest.

お母かあさんと、4匹ひきの子こうさぎです。

Okāsan to, yon-hiki no ko-usagi desu.

There is a mother and four baby rabbits.

Vocabulary
  • 家族かぞく (kazoku) — family
  • 子こうさぎ (kōusagi) — baby rabbit
  • お母かあさん (okāsan) — mother; mom
Grammar Notes
  • N no N — Links two nouns; the first noun describes or belongs to the second, as in “the family of rabbits.”
  • N ga imasu — Imasu states that a person or animal exists or is present; ga marks that subject.

Vocabulary

  • 外そと (soto) — outside
  • 遊あそぶ (asobu) — to play
  • 行いく (iku) — to go; includes the set expressions for leaving and returning
  • 家いえ (ie) — house; home
  • 怖こわい (kowai) — scary; frightening

Story page 1616 / 20
ピーターは、木戸きどまで走はしります！

Pītā wa, kido made hashirimasu!

Grammar Notes
  • N made — Made marks an endpoint, meaning “to” or “until.”
  • N mo — Mo means “also” or “too,” replacing the topic marker wa in these sentences.
Story page 1717 / 20
ピーターは家いえに帰かえりました。

Pītā wa ie ni kaerimashita.

Peter went home.

とても疲つかれましたから、すぐ寝ねました。

Totemo tsukaremashita kara, sugu nemashita.

He was very tired, so he went straight to sleep.

「あれ？服ふくは？」

“Are? Fuku wa?”

“Huh? Where are my clothes?”

Vocabulary
  • 疲つかれる (tsukareru) — to get tired
  • 寝ねる (neru) — to sleep; to go to bed
  • 服ふく (fuku) — clothes
Grammar Notes
  • V-ta kara — Kara after a past-form verb gives a reason: “because” or “so.”
