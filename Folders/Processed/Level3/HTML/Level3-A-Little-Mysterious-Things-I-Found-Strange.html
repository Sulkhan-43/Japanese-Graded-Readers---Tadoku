Story summary

Gyu made a book about things in Japan that he found strange.

Masks1 / 6
マスク

Masuku

Masks

これははマスクです。

Kore wa masuku desu.

This is a mask.

日本にほんではは、夏なつでもマスクをつける人ひとが多おおいです。

Nihon de wa, natsu demo masuku o tsukeru hito ga ōi desu.

In Japan, many people wear masks even in summer.

その理由りゆうを知しりたくて、日本人にほんじんの友達ともだちに聞ききました。

Sono riyū o shiritakute, Nihonjin no tomodachi ni kikimashita.

Wanting to know the reason, I asked a Japanese friend.

Vocabulary
  • マスク (masuku) — mask
  • 夏なつ (natsu) — summer
  • 人ひと (hito) — person; people
  • 多おおい (ōi) — many; numerous
  • 理由りゆう (riyū) — reason
  • 知しる (shiru) — to know; find out
  • 日本人にほんじん (Nihonjin) — Japanese person; Japanese people
  • 友達ともだち (tomodachi) — friend
  • 聞きく (kiku) — to ask; listen
Grammar Notes
  • N de wa — This pattern sets a place or context as the topic, often translated as “in” or “at” that context.
  • N demo — This pattern means “even” when presenting a surprising or inclusive example.
  • V-takute — This pattern connects a desire to a following action or result, often meaning “wanting to do.”
Why people wear masks2 / 6
「今日きょうは化粧けしょうをする時間じかんがなくて、顔かおを隠かくしているの」と言いわれました。

“Kyō wa keshō o suru jikan ga nakute, kao o kakushite iru no” to iwaremashita.

“I didn’t have time to put on makeup today, so I’m hiding my face,” I was told.

ほかにも、夏なつでも風邪かぜをひかないようにマスクをするそうです。

Hoka ni mo, natsu demo kaze o hikanai yō ni masuku o suru sō desu.

I hear people also wear masks so they won’t catch a cold, even in summer.

Vocabulary
  • 化粧けしょう (keshō) — makeup
  • 時間じかん (jikan) — time
  • 顔かお (kao) — face
  • 隠かくす (kakusu) — to hide
  • 言いう (iu) — to say; tell
  • 風邪かぜ (kaze) — a cold
Grammar Notes
  • V-nakute — This pattern connects a negative state or action to what follows, often giving a reason or circumstance.
  • V-nai yō ni — This pattern expresses a purpose or precaution: doing something so that an unwanted event does not happen.
  • sō da (hearsay) — This pattern reports information heard from someone else, similar to “I hear that” or “apparently.”
Makeup3 / 6
化粧けしょう

Keshō

Makeup

日本にほんではは、高校生こうこうせいから80歳さいの女おんなの人ひとまで、みんな化粧けしょうをしています。

Nihon de wa, kōkōsei kara hassissai no onna no hito made, minna keshō o shite imasu.

In Japan, everyone from high school students to 80-year-old women wears makeup.

それはは不思議ふしぎだと思おもいました。

Sore wa fushigi da to omoimashita.

I thought that was strange.

Vocabulary
  • 高校生こうこうせい (kōkōsei) — high school student
  • 歳さい (sai) — years old; age counter
  • 女おんな (onna) — woman
  • 不思議ふしぎ (fushigi) — strange; mysterious
  • 思おもう (omou) — to think
Grammar Notes
  • N kara N made — This pattern marks the starting and ending points of a range, meaning “from N to N.”
  • to omou — This pattern introduces a thought or opinion, similar to “I think that.”
A tradition from the Heian period4 / 6
実じつは、日本にほんの化粧けしょうは、平安時代へいあんじだいから伝つたわってきた文化ぶんかです。

Jitsu wa, Nihon no keshō wa, Heian jidai kara tsutawatte kita bunka desu.

In fact, makeup in Japan is a tradition passed down from the Heian period.

※平安時代へいあんじだいは794年ななひゃくきゅうじゅうよねん～1185年せんひゃくはちじゅうごねん

* Heian jidai wa 794-nen–1185-nen.

The Heian period lasted from 794 to 1185.

今いまではは、マナーになりました。

Ima de wa, manā ni narimashita.

Nowadays, it has become a matter of etiquette.

Vocabulary
  • 実じつ (jitsu) — actually; in fact (as a sentence opener)
  • 平安時代へいあんじだい (Heian jidai) — Heian period
  • 伝つたわる (tsutawaru) — to be passed down
  • 文化ぶんか (bunka) — culture; tradition
  • 今いま (ima) — now; nowadays
  • マナー (manā) — manners; etiquette
Grammar Notes
  • V-te kuru — This pattern describes an action or change that began in the past and has continued toward the present.
  • N ni naru — This pattern describes a change of state, meaning “to become N.”
The entranceway5 / 6
玄関げんかん

Genkan

The entranceway

これはは玄関げんかんです。

Kore wa genkan desu.

This is the entranceway.

日本にほんの家いえに入はいるとき、ここで靴くつを脱ぬぎます。

Nihon no ie ni hairu toki, koko de kutsu o nugimasu.

When you enter a Japanese home, you take off your shoes here.

玄関げんかんにはは段差だんさがあります。

Genkan ni wa dansa ga arimasu.

There is a step at the entranceway.

マンガまんがやアニメあにめでもよく見みる場所ばしょです。

Manga ya anime demo yoku miru basho desu.

It’s a place you often see in manga and anime.

Vocabulary
  • 玄関げんかん (genkan) — entranceway; entry hall
  • 家いえ (ie) — house; home
  • 入はいる (hairu) — to enter
  • 靴くつ (kutsu) — shoes
  • 脱ぬぐ (nugu) — to take off (clothing or shoes)
  • 段差だんさ (dansa) — step; difference in floor level
  • マンガ (manga) — manga; comic
  • アニメ (anime) — anime
  • 見みる (miru) — to see; watch
  • 場所ばしょ (basho) — place; location
Grammar Notes
  • V-dictionary toki — This pattern uses a verb to specify when an action takes place, meaning “when doing.”
Why the step matters6 / 6
実じつは、玄関げんかんの段差だんさは、日本人にほんじんにとって大切たいせつなものです。

Jitsu wa, genkan no dansa wa, Nihonjin ni totte taisetsu na mono desu.

Actually, the step at the entranceway is important to Japanese people.

外そとと内うちの気持きもちが変かわるところです。

Soto to uchi no kimochi ga kawaru tokoro desu.

It is a place where the feeling of being outside or inside changes.

入はいるだけでリラックスできます。

Hairu dake de rirakkusu dekimasu.

Just entering is enough to help you relax.

Vocabulary
  • 大切たいせつ (taisetsu) — important; precious
  • 外そと (soto) — outside
  • 内うち (uchi) — inside
  • 気持きもち (kimochi) — feeling; mood
  • 変かわる (kawaru) — to change
  • リラックス (rirakkusu) — to relax; relaxation
Grammar Notes
  • N ni totte — This pattern marks the person or group for whom something is important or relevant, meaning “for N.”
  • V-dictionary dake de — This pattern means “just by doing” and describes a result achieved with only that action.
