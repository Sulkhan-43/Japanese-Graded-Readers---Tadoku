ある朝あさ、子こぎつねが外そとへ出でようとして、

Yuki o hajimete mita ko-gitsune wa, yuki ga, ohisama no hikari de kirakira hikatte iru no o mite, “Me ni nanika ga haitta!” to machigaeta no deshō.

Seeing snow for the first time, the little fox must have mistaken its sparkling in the sunlight for something getting into his eyes.

Vocabulary
  • 朝あさ (asa) — morning
  • 外そと (soto) — outside
  • 痛いたい (itai) — painful; hurting
  • びっくりする (bikkuri suru) — to be surprised or startled
  • 真まっ白しろ (masshiro) — pure white; completely white
  • 昨日きのう (kinō) — yesterday
  • 雪ゆき (yuki) — snow
  • 降ふる (furu) — to fall, of rain or snow
  • 初はじめて (hajimete) — for the first time
  • 光ひかる (hikaru) — to shine; to sparkle
Grammar Notes
  • ～ようとする — This pattern combines a verb's volitional form with a verb meaning to do. It expresses trying to act or being about to act.
  • ～てみる — This pattern follows a verb's connecting form and means to try an action to see what happens.
  • ～でしょう — This pattern expresses a probable explanation or inference. Here the narrator suggests what the little fox probably thought.

Grammar Notes

  • 左手ひだりて (hidarite) — left hand
  • 触さわる (sawaru) — to touch
  • 変かわる (kawaru) — to change; to turn into
  • 探さがす (sagasu) — to look for; to search for
  • 叩たたく (tataku) — to knock; to tap; to strike
  • 渡わたす (watasu) — to hand over; to pass to someone
Grammar Notes
  • ～たら — This pattern forms a condition meaning if or when. Here it introduces what to do after finding the shop or seeing a fox.
  • ～なさい — This pattern attaches to a verb stem to give an instruction, often from a parent or teacher to a child.
