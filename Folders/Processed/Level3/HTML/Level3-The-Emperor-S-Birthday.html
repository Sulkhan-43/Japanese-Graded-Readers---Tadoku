The Emperor's Birthday

天皇てんのうの誕生日たんじょうび

Page 1: A Birthday Visit1 / 6
12月がつ23日にちは天皇てんのうの誕生日たんじょうびです。

Jūni-gatsu nijūsan-nichi wa tennō no tanjōbi desu.

December 23 is the emperor's birthday.

その日ひは国くにの祝日しゅくじつです。

Sono hi wa kuni no shukujitsu desu.

That day is a national holiday.

天皇てんのうは東京とうきょうの皇居こうきょで短みじかい話はなしをするから、みんなが皇居こうきょに入はいれます。

Tennō wa Tōkyō no kōkyo de mijikai hanashi o suru kara, minna ga kōkyo ni hairemasu.

The emperor gives a short speech at the Imperial Palace in Tokyo, so everyone can enter the palace grounds.

ほかの日ひには皇居こうきょの中なかを見みられません。

Hoka no hi ni wa kōkyo no naka o miraremasen.

On other days, you cannot see inside the palace grounds.

2014年ねん12月がつ23日にちは天皇てんのうの81歳さいの誕生日たんじょうびでした。

Nisen jūyon-nen jūni-gatsu nijūsan-nichi wa tennō no hachijūissai no tanjōbi deshita.

December 23, 2014, was the emperor's eighty-first birthday.

僕ぼくは皇居こうきょに行いきました。

Boku wa kōkyo ni ikimashita.

I went to the Imperial Palace.

天皇てんのうは午前中ごぜんちゅうに話はなしを3回かいしました。

Tennō wa gozenchū ni hanashi o san-kai shimashita.

The emperor gave his speech three times during the morning.

僕ぼくは最後さいごの回かいに行いきました。

Boku wa saigo no kai ni ikimashita.

I went to the last session.

Vocabulary
  • 天皇てんのう (tennō) — emperor of Japan
  • 誕生日たんじょうび (tanjōbi) — birthday
  • 祝日しゅくじつ (shukujitsu) — public holiday
  • 東京とうきょう (Tōkyō) — Tokyo
  • 皇居こうきょ (kōkyo) — Imperial Palace and its grounds
  • 短みじかい (mijikai) — short
  • 話はなし (hanashi) — talk; speech
  • 入はいる (hairu) — enter; go in
  • ほか (hoka) — other; another
  • 午前中ごぜんちゅう (gozenchū) — during the morning
  • 最後さいご (saigo) — last; final
Grammar Notes
  • Nで — This pattern marks the place where an action happens.
  • plain clause + から — This pattern gives a reason or cause, meaning because or so.
  • V-える / V-られる — This pattern expresses the ability or opportunity to do something. Its negative form means cannot.
Page 2: Crowds and Security2 / 6
大手町おおてまち駅えきは込こんでいました。

Ōtemachi-eki wa konde imashita.

Otemachi Station was crowded.

駅えきから皇居こうきょまで長ながい人々ひとびとの列れつがありました。

Eki kara kōkyo made nagai hitobito no retsu ga arimashita.

There was a long line of people from the station to the Imperial Palace.

みんなは天皇てんのうを見みたい人ひとでした。

Minna wa tennō o mitai hito deshita.

They were all people who wanted to see the emperor.

警察官けいさつかんも多おおかったです。

Keisatsukan mo ōkatta desu.

There were also many police officers.

皇居こうきょの門もんの前まえに荷物にもつのチェックポイントがありました。

Kōkyo no mon no mae ni nimotsu no chekkupointo ga arimashita.

There was a baggage checkpoint in front of the palace gate.

警察官けいさつかんはみんなのかばんの中なかを見みました。

Keisatsukan wa minna no kaban no naka o mimashita.

The police officers looked inside everyone's bags.

僕ぼくはかばんを持もってなかったから、チェックポイントで待またなくてもよかったです。

Boku wa kaban o motte nakatta kara, chekkupointo de matanakute mo yokatta desu.

I did not have a bag, so I did not have to wait at the checkpoint.

Vocabulary
  • 大手町おおてまち (Ōtemachi) — Otemachi, a place name in Tokyo
  • 駅えき (eki) — train station
  • 込こむ (komu) — become crowded
  • 長ながい (nagai) — long
  • 人々ひとびと (hitobito) — people
  • 列れつ (retsu) — line; queue
  • 警察官けいさつかん (keisatsukan) — police officer
  • 多おおい (ōi) — many; numerous
  • 門もん (mon) — gate
  • 前まえ (mae) — front; in front of
  • 荷物にもつ (nimotsu) — baggage; belongings
  • チェックポイント (chekkupointo) — checkpoint
  • かばん (kaban) — bag
  • 持もつ (motsu) — hold; carry; have with oneself
  • 待まつ (matsu) — wait
Grammar Notes
  • V-ている — This pattern describes an ongoing action or continuing state. The past form describes what was happening or how things were. The informal negative past can omit the initial vowel of the auxiliary.
  • NからNまで — This pattern marks a starting point and an endpoint, meaning from one place to another.
  • Nがある — This pattern states that an inanimate thing exists or is present. A location can precede it.
  • V-たい — This pattern attaches to a verb stem to express wanting to do the action.
  • plain verb clause + N — This pattern places a descriptive verb clause directly before a noun, expressing meanings such as people who want to see something or flags that someone received.
  • V-なくてもよい — This pattern means that an action is unnecessary. In the past, it means did not have to do something.
Page 3: Inside the Grounds3 / 6
皇居こうきょの門もんに入はいりました。

Kōkyo no mon ni hairimashita.

I went through the palace gate.

中なかに大おおきくてきれいな公園こうえんがありました。

Naka ni ōkikute kirei na kōen ga arimashita.

Inside, there was a large, beautiful park.

でも公園こうえんを探検たんけんすることができませんでした。

Demo kōen o tanken suru koto ga dekimasen deshita.

But I could not explore the park.

みんなは一本いっぽんの道みちだけ歩あるかなければなりませんでした。

Minna wa ippon no michi dake arukanakereba narimasen deshita.

Everyone had to walk along just one path.

ときどき止とまって写真しゃしんをとりました。

Tokidoki tomatte shashin o torimashita.

I stopped from time to time and took photographs.

色々いろいろな建物たてものとか古ふるい壁かべなどを見みました。

Iroiro na tatemono toka furui kabe nado o mimashita.

I saw various buildings, old walls, and other things.

Vocabulary
  • 大おおきい (ōkii) — large; big
  • きれい (kirei) — beautiful; clean
  • 公園こうえん (kōen) — park
  • 探検たんけんする (tanken suru) — explore
  • 一本いっぽん (ippon) — one long object; here, one path
  • 道みち (michi) — road; path
  • 歩あるく (aruku) — walk
  • ときどき (tokidoki) — sometimes; from time to time
  • 止とまる (tomaru) — stop
  • 写真しゃしん (shashin) — photograph
  • とる (toru) — take a photograph
  • 色々いろいろ (iroiro) — various; a variety of
  • 建物たてもの (tatemono) — building
  • 古ふるい (furui) — old
  • 壁かべ (kabe) — wall
Grammar Notes
  • i-adjective-くて — This pattern links an adjective to another description, meaning that something has both qualities.
  • V-ることができる — This pattern follows a dictionary-form verb to express being able to perform an action. The negative past means could not.
  • Nだけ — This pattern limits the statement to the preceding item, meaning only or just.
  • V-なければならない — This pattern expresses obligation, meaning must or have to. Its polite past form means had to.
  • V-て + V — This pattern links actions performed in sequence or together. The final verb supplies the sentence's tense.
  • NとかNなど — This pattern lists examples without implying that the list is complete.
Page 4: Waiting at the Palace4 / 6
すぐにほかの門もんに入はいりました。

Sugu ni hoka no mon ni hairimashita.

I soon went through another gate.

そこに宮殿きゅうでんの前まえの広場ひろばがありました。

Soko ni kyūden no mae no hiroba ga arimashita.

There was a plaza there in front of the palace.

その宮殿きゅうでんの一部分いちぶぶんは新あたらしいガラスのビルです。

Sono kyūden no ichibubun wa atarashii garasu no biru desu.

One part of that palace is a new glass building.

広場ひろばはすごく込こんでいました。

Hiroba wa sugoku konde imashita.

The plaza was very crowded.

みんなは天皇てんのうを待まっていました。

Minna wa tennō o matte imashita.

Everyone was waiting for the emperor.

たくさんの外国人がいこくじんもいました。

Takusan no gaikokujin mo imashita.

There were also many foreigners.

たくさんの人ひとはチェックポイントでもらった日の丸ひのまるを持もっていました。

Takusan no hito wa chekkupointo de moratta hinomaru o motte imashita.

Many people were holding Japanese flags they had received at the checkpoint.

Vocabulary
  • すぐ (sugu) — soon; immediately
  • 宮殿きゅうでん (kyūden) — palace building
  • 広場ひろば (hiroba) — plaza; open square
  • 一部分いちぶぶん (ichibubun) — one part; a portion
  • 新あたらしい (atarashii) — new
  • ガラス (garasu) — glass
  • ビル (biru) — building, especially a modern substantial building
  • すごく (sugoku) — very; extremely
  • たくさん (takusan) — many; a lot
  • 外国人がいこくじん (gaikokujin) — foreigner; person from another country
  • もらう (morau) — receive
  • 日ひの丸まる (hinomaru) — Japanese national flag
Page 5: The Emperor Appears5 / 6
11時じ40分ふんに天皇てんのうと家族かぞくは宮殿きゅうでんの2階かいに現あらわれました。

Jūichi-ji yonjuppun ni tennō to kazoku wa kyūden no ni-kai ni arawaremashita.

At 11:40, the emperor and his family appeared on the second floor of the palace.

広場ひろばの人々ひとびとは何回なんかいも「天皇陛下てんのうへいか、万歳ばんざい！」と叫さけんで日の丸ひのまるを振ふりました。

Hiroba no hitobito wa nan-kai mo "Tennō heika, banzai!" to sakende hinomaru o furimashita.

The people in the plaza repeatedly shouted, "Long live His Majesty the Emperor!" and waved Japanese flags.

天皇てんのうがマイクのところに行いくと、みんなは静しずかになりました。

Tennō ga maiku no tokoro ni iku to, minna wa shizuka ni narimashita.

When the emperor went to the microphone, everyone became quiet.

天皇てんのうは短みじかい話はなしをしました。

Tennō wa mijikai hanashi o shimashita.

The emperor gave a short speech.

平和へいわについてだと思おもいますが、天皇てんのうの日本語にほんごはちょっと難むずかしかったです。

Heiwa ni tsuite da to omoimasu ga, tennō no Nihongo wa chotto muzukashikatta desu.

I think it was about peace, but the emperor's Japanese was a little difficult.

話はなしの後あとにもう1回かいみんなは「天皇陛下てんのうへいか、万歳ばんざい！」と叫さけびました。

Hanashi no ato ni mō ikkai minna wa "Tennō heika, banzai!" to sakebimashita.

After the speech, everyone shouted, "Long live His Majesty the Emperor!" once more.

Vocabulary
  • 家族かぞく (kazoku) — family
  • 現あらわれる (arawareru) — appear; come into view
  • 何回なんかい (nan-kai) — how many times; with an additive particle, many times
  • 天皇陛下てんのうへいか (tennō heika) — His Majesty the Emperor
  • 万歳ばんざい (banzai) — long live; a celebratory cheer
  • 叫さけぶ (sakebu) — shout; cry out
  • 振ふる (furu) — wave; shake
  • マイク (maiku) — microphone
  • ところ (tokoro) — place; spot
  • 静しずか (shizuka) — quiet
  • 平和へいわ (heiwa) — peace
  • 思おもう (omou) — think; believe
  • 日本語にほんご (Nihongo) — Japanese language
  • ちょっと (chotto) — a little; somewhat
  • 難むずかしい (muzukashii) — difficult
  • 後あと (ato) — after; afterward
  • もう (mō) — again; another, before a quantity
Grammar Notes
  • 何なん + counter + も — This pattern combines a question word, a counter, and an additive particle to mean many or repeated instances rather than ask a question.
  • quotation + と — This pattern marks the words spoken before a verb of speaking or shouting.
  • V-ると — This pattern introduces an event followed by a natural or observed result, meaning when or once.
  • na-adjective + になる — This pattern describes a change into the state expressed by an adjective, meaning become.
  • Nについて — This pattern identifies a topic, meaning about or concerning.
  • plain clause + と思おもう — This pattern presents a statement as the speaker's thought or belief, meaning I think that.
  • clause + が — This pattern connects contrasting clauses, meaning but.

Yukkuri deguchi no mon ni aruite takusan no shashin o torimashita.

I walked slowly toward the exit gate and took lots of photographs.

  • 出口でぐち (deguchi) — exit
