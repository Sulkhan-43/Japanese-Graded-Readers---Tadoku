The Happy Prince

幸しあわせな王子おうじ

Repeated vocabulary and grammar explanations are intentionally omitted after their first useful explanation.

Story summary

In a certain town stood a statue of the Happy Prince. While a swallow was resting there, a drop of water fell. Looking up, the swallow saw that the Happy Prince was crying... A heartwarming classic by Oscar Wilde.

ある町まちに、「幸しあわせな王子おうじ」の像ぞうがありました。

Aru machi ni, 'Shiawase na Ōji' no zō ga arimashita.

In a certain town stood a statue of the Happy Prince.

王子おうじの像ぞうは、高たかい台だいの上うえに立たっていました。

Ōji no zō wa, takai dai no ue ni tatte imashita.

The prince's statue stood on a high pedestal.

体からだは金色きんいろです。

Karada wa kin'iro desu.

His body was gold.

目めはきれいな青あおです。

Me wa kirei na ao desu.

His eyes were a beautiful blue.

サファイアという宝石ほうせきです。

Saf faia to iu hōseki desu.

They were gemstones called sapphires.

剣けんには、赤あかい宝石ほうせき、ルビーがついていました。

Ken ni wa, akai hōseki, rubī ga tsuite imashita.

A red gemstone, a ruby, was attached to his sword.

町まちの人ひとはみんな、このピカピカ光ひかるきれいな「幸しあわせな王子おうじ」が大好だいすきでした。

Machi no hito wa minna, kono pikapika hikaru kirei na 'Shiawase na Ōji' ga daisuki deshita.

Everyone in town loved this beautiful, shining Happy Prince.

いつも、この「幸しあわせな王子おうじ」を見みながら、幸しあわせな気持きもちになるのでした。

Itsumo, kono 'Shiawase na Ōji' o minagara, shiawase na kimochi ni naru no deshita.

Whenever they looked at the Happy Prince, they felt happy.

Vocabulary

Grammar Notes
  • という — This pattern introduces a name or label: a gemstone called sapphire.
  • ながら — This pattern follows a verb stem and links two actions performed simultaneously by the same subject.
A Swallow Arrives2 / 13
ある日ひの夕方ゆうがた、この町まちに、つばめが飛とんできました。

Aru hi no yūgata, kono machi ni, tsubame ga tonde kimashita.

だんだん寒さむくなってきたので、これから、暖あたたかい南みなみの国くにへ行いくのです。

It was gradually getting colder, so he was now heading to a warm southern country.

Samui na.

It's cold.

暗くらくなってきた。

Kuraku natte kita.

It's getting dark.

どこか寝ねるところはないかな——

Dokoka neru tokoro wa nai kana.

I wonder if there's somewhere to sleep.

つばめがそう思おもいながら飛とんでいると、幸しあわせな王子おうじの像ぞうが見みえました。

A, asoko ga ii!

Ah, that looks good!

あそこで寝ねよう」

Asoko de neyō.

I'll sleep there.

Grammar Notes

Grammar Notes
  • たことがある — This pattern describes past experience. Here its negative form, with an expression meaning not even once, states that the prince had never cried.
  • てから — This pattern places the following action or state after the preceding action: after doing something or since doing it.
  • ようだ — This pattern expresses an inference from available evidence: someone seems or appears to be in a particular state.
  • てくれませんか — This pattern politely asks someone to perform an action for the speaker or someone the speaker cares about.

Grammar Notes
  • のに — This pattern means although or even though and introduces a result contrary to expectation.
  • のために・のための — This pattern identifies the person or group benefiting from an action or object. The adverbial form modifies an action; the attributive form modifies a noun.
  • てしまう — This pattern presents an action or change as completed, often with a sense of regret or an unwanted result. It can also emphasize finishing an action completely.
  • なければならない — This pattern attaches to a negative verb stem and expresses obligation: must or have to.
  • ことができる — This pattern follows a dictionary-form verb and expresses ability or possibility. The negative form means cannot; the topic particle can replace the subject particle for emphasis.

Vocabulary
  • 珍めずらしい (mezurashii) — rare; unusual
  • 通とおり (tōri) — the way; as directed

Grammar Notes
  • 通とおりに — This pattern means in the way specified by the preceding phrase. Here the swallow is asked to act exactly as the prince says.
  • たまま — This pattern indicates that a state resulting from an action continues during another action: the man sleeps while still holding his pen.
  • だろう — This pattern expresses a conjecture or prediction. With a question ending, it conveys wondering whether something is true.
