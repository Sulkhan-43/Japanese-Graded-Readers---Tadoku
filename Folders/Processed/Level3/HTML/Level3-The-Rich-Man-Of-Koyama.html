The Pond1 / 15
これは池いけです。

Kore wa ike desu.

This is a pond.

池いけの名前なまえは「湖山池こやまいけ」です。

Ike no namae wa "Koyamaike" desu.

The pond is called Koyama Pond.

Vocabulary
  • 湖山長者こやまちょうじゃ (Koyama Chōja) — the Rich Man of Koyama; the man's local name
  • ざんねん (zannen) — disappointing; unfortunate
  • お金持かねもち (okanemochi) — a rich person
  • 池いけ (ike) — pond
  • 名前なまえ (namae) — name
  • 湖山池こやまいけ (Koyamaike) — Koyama Pond
Once a Rice Field2 / 15
湖山池こやまいけはむかし、広ひろい田たんぼでした。

Koyamaike wa mukashi, hiroi tanbo deshita.

Koyama Pond was once a large rice field.

ある日ひ、田たんぼが全部ぜんぶ、池いけになりました。

Aru hi, tanbo ga zenbu, ike ni narimashita.

One day, the entire rice field became a pond.

なぜ、池いけになったのでしょうか。

Naze, ike ni natta no deshō ka.

Why did it become a pond?

これは鳥取県とっとりけんのむかし話ばなしです。

Kore wa Tottori-ken no mukashibanashi desu.

This is a folktale from Tottori Prefecture.

Vocabulary
  • むかし (mukashi) — long ago; in the past
  • 広ひろい (hiroi) — wide; spacious; large in area
  • 田たんぼ (tanbo) — rice field
  • 全部ぜんぶ (zenbu) — all; the whole of something
  • なる (naru) — to become
  • 鳥取県とっとりけん (Tottori-ken) — Tottori Prefecture
  • むかし話ばなし (mukashibanashi) — folktale; tale from long ago
Grammar Notes
  • Nになる — This pattern expresses becoming something or changing into a new state named by a noun.
  • ～のでしょうか — This pattern asks reflectively for an explanation, often meaning that the narrator wonders why something happened.
The Rich Man3 / 15
むかし、お金持かねもちがいました。

Mukashi, okanemochi ga imashita.

Long ago, there was a rich man.

このお金持かねもちは、広ひろい田たんぼと、りっぱな家いえと、金きん、銀ぎんをたくさん持もっていました。

Kono okanemochi wa, hiroi tanbo to, rippa na ie to, kin, gin o takusan motte imashita.

This rich man owned a large rice field, a splendid house, and plenty of gold and silver.

村むらの人ひとたちは、このお金持かねもちを「湖山長者こやまちょうじゃ」と呼よんでいました。

Mura no hitotachi wa, kono okanemochi o "Koyama Chōja" to yonde imashita.

The villagers called this rich man the Rich Man of Koyama.

Vocabulary
  • りっぱ (rippa) — splendid; impressive
  • 金きん (kin) — gold
  • 銀ぎん (gin) — silver
  • たくさん (takusan) — many; much; plenty
  • 持もつ (motsu) — to hold; to possess; to carry
  • 村むら (mura) — village
  • 呼よぶ (yobu) — to call; to name
Grammar Notes
  • ～ている — This pattern describes an ongoing action or a continuing state resulting from an action. Past and negative forms retain this distinction.
  • ～と呼よぶ — This pattern introduces the name or title by which someone or something is called.
A Family Rule4 / 15
日本にほんでは、春はるに田植たうえをして、米こめを作つくります。

Nihon de wa, haru ni taue o shite, kome o tsukurimasu.

In Japan, people plant rice seedlings in spring to grow rice.

長者ちょうじゃの家いえでは、「一日いちにちで田植たうえを終おえる」と、むかしから決きまっていました。

Chōja no ie de wa, "Ichinichi de taue o oeru" to, mukashi kara kimatte imashita.

In the rich man's household, it had long been the rule to finish planting rice in one day.

毎年まいとし、長者ちょうじゃはたくさんの人ひとを使つかって広ひろい田たんぼの田植たうえをしましたので、田植たうえは一日いちにちで終おわっていました。

Maitoshi, chōja wa takusan no hito o tsukatte hiroi tanbo no taue o shimashita node, taue wa ichinichi de owatte imashita.

Every year, the rich man employed many people to plant his large rice field, so the planting was finished in one day.

Vocabulary
  • 田植たうえ (taue) — planting rice seedlings
  • 長者ちょうじゃ (chōja) — a wealthy man, especially in a folktale
  • 一日いちにち (ichinichi) — one day
  • 終おえる (oeru) — to finish something
  • 決きまる (kimaru) — to be decided; to be established
  • 毎年まいとし (maitoshi) — every year
  • 使つかう (tsukau) — to use; here, to employ people
  • 終おわる (owaru) — to end; to be finished
Grammar Notes
  • durationで — This pattern marks the amount of time needed or allowed to complete an action.
  • ～と決きまっている — This pattern presents a quoted action or statement as an established rule or decision.
  • ～ので — This pattern connects a reason or cause to its result.
Planting Begins5 / 15
今年ことしも田植たうえが始はじまりました。

Kotoshi mo taue ga hajimarimashita.

Rice planting began again that year.

村むらの人ひとがたくさん手伝てつだいます。

Mura no hito ga takusan tetsudaimasu.

Many villagers help.

みんな朝あさ早はやくから働はたらきます。

Minna asa hayaku kara hatarakimasu.

Everyone works from early in the morning.

長者ちょうじゃは、今年ことしも一日いちにちで田植たうえが終おわると考かんがえていました。

Chōja wa, kotoshi mo ichinichi de taue ga owaru to kangaete imashita.

The rich man thought the rice planting would be finished in one day that year too.

Vocabulary
  • 今年ことし (kotoshi) — this year
  • 始はじまる (hajimaru) — to begin
  • 手伝てつだう (tetsudau) — to help with a task
  • 早はやい (hayai) — early
  • 働はたらく (hataraku) — to work
  • 考かんがえる (kangaeru) — to think; to consider
Grammar Notes
  • ～と考かんがえる — This pattern marks the preceding statement as the content of someone's thought.
The Monkeys6 / 15
その日ひの昼ひる、近ちかくの山やまからサルが来きました。

Sono hi no hiru, chikaku no yama kara saru ga kimashita.

At midday that day, monkeys came from a nearby mountain.

お母かあさんのサルと子こどものサルでした。

Okāsan no saru to kodomo no saru deshita.

They were a mother monkey and her child.

一人ひとりの女おんなの人ひとがサルを見みつけました。

Hitori no onna no hito ga saru o mitsukemashita.

One woman spotted the monkeys.

そして「サルだ、サルだ」と大おおきい声こえで言いいました。

Soshite "Saru da, saru da" to ōkii koe de iimashita.

Then she called out loudly, "Monkeys! Monkeys!"

Vocabulary
  • 近ちかく (chikaku) — nearby; the vicinity
  • サル (saru) — monkey
  • お母かあさん (okāsan) — mother
  • 子こども (kodomo) — child
  • 一人ひとり (hitori) — one person
  • 見みつける (mitsukeru) — to find; to spot
  • 声こえ (koe) — voice
Grammar Notes
  • ～と言いう — This pattern marks preceding words as what someone says. The speaking verb may be inflected or joined to another action.
A Costly Distraction7 / 15
サルはびっくりして走はしり始はじめました。

Saru wa bikkuri shite hashirihajimemashita.

The monkeys were startled and began to run.

みんなは、サルが遠とおくの山やまに帰かえるまで見みていました。

Minna wa, saru ga tōku no yama ni kaeru made mite imashita.

Everyone watched until the monkeys returned to the distant mountain.

サルを見みたのは少すこしの時間じかんでした。

Saru o mita no wa sukoshi no jikan deshita.

They watched the monkeys for only a short time.

しかし、仕事しごとは大おおきく遅おくれました。

Shikashi, shigoto wa ōkiku okuremashita.

However, the work fell far behind.

Vocabulary
  • びっくりする (bikkuri suru) — to be surprised; to be startled
  • 走はしる (hashiru) — to run
  • 遠とおく (tōku) — far away; a distant place
  • 帰かえる (kaeru) — to return home
  • 少すこし (sukoshi) — a little; a short amount
  • 時間じかん (jikan) — time; a period of time
  • 仕事しごと (shigoto) — work
  • 遅おくれる (okureru) — to be delayed; to fall behind
Grammar Notes
  • ～始はじめる — This pattern attaches to a verb stem to express beginning that action.
  • ～まで — This pattern follows a clause to mean until the event or endpoint described in that clause.
  • verbの — This pattern turns an action clause into a noun phrase so it can serve as a topic or an object.
The Rich Man Turns Pale8 / 15
夕方ゆうがた、長者ちょうじゃが田たんぼに来きたとき、田植たうえは、まだ終おわっていませんでした。

Yūgata, chōja ga tanbo ni kita toki, taue wa, mada owatte imasen deshita.

When the rich man came to the rice field that evening, the planting was still unfinished.

長者ちょうじゃは青あおくなりました。

Chōja wa aoku narimashita.

The rich man turned pale.

「家いえの決きまりは一日いちにちで田植たうえを終おえること。

"Ie no kimari wa ichinichi de taue o oeru koto.

"Our family rule is to finish planting rice in one day.

これでは今年ことしは田植たうえが終おわらない」

Kore de wa kotoshi wa taue ga owaranai."

At this rate, we will not finish the rice planting this year."

Vocabulary
  • 夕方ゆうがた (yūgata) — late afternoon; early evening
  • 青あおい (aoi) — blue; here, pale from shock or fear
  • 決きまり (kimari) — rule; established custom
Grammar Notes
  • ～とき — This pattern introduces the time when an action or state occurs. The preceding verb form helps show its timing.
  • まだ～ていない — This pattern means that an expected action has not happened or been completed yet. Here it is in the past.
  • adjectiveくなる — This pattern uses the adverbial form of an adjective to express becoming that way.
  • verbこと — This pattern turns a verb clause into an abstract action or requirement. Here it identifies the content of a family rule.
The Golden Fan9 / 15
「よし、お日ひさまをもどして、夜よるになるのを遅おそくしよう」と言いいました。

"Yoshi, ohisama o modoshite, yoru ni naru no o osoku shiyō" to iimashita.

"All right, I will bring the sun back and delay nightfall," he said.

長者ちょうじゃは家いえから金きんのおうぎを持もってきました。

Chōja wa ie kara kin no ōgi o motte kimashita.

The rich man brought a golden fan from his house.

Vocabulary
  • お日ひさま (ohisama) — the sun, addressed affectionately or respectfully
  • もどす (modosu) — to bring something back; to return something
  • 遅おそい (osoi) — late; slow
  • おうぎ (ōgi) — folding fan
Grammar Notes
  • ～よう／～おう — This pattern expresses the speaker's intention or proposes an action to others, meaning either I will or let us.
  • ～てくる — This pattern describes an action or movement approaching the speaker or the scene being described, including bringing something there.
The Sun Returns10 / 15
そして、お日ひさまに「もどれ、もどれ。

Soshite, ohisama ni "Modore, modore.

Then he addressed the sun: "Come back, come back.

田植たうえが終おわるまでそこにいろ」と言いいながら、おうぎを三回さんかいふりました。

Taue ga owaru made soko ni iro" to iinagara, ōgi o sankai furimashita.

Stay there until the rice planting is finished," he said, waving his fan three times.

すると、お日ひさまがすっすっすっと高たかくもどりました。

Suruto, ohisama ga sussussutto takaku modorimashita.

Then the sun swiftly rose back up high.

Vocabulary
  • もどる (modoru) — to return; to go back
  • 三回さんかい (sankai) — three times
  • ふる (furu) — to wave; to swing
  • 高たかい (takai) — high
Grammar Notes
  • 命令形めいれいけい — This pattern gives a direct, forceful command. The rich man uses it to order the sun to return and stay.
  • ～ながら — This pattern attaches to a verb stem to show that the same person performs two actions at the same time.
  • すると — This pattern introduces something that happens immediately after, or as a result of, the preceding event.

Water in the Night13 / 15
みんながお酒さけを飲のんでいるとき、外そとでは大変たいへんなことが起おこっていました。

Minna ga osake o nonde iru toki, soto de wa taihen na koto ga okotte imashita.

While everyone was drinking sake, something terrible was happening outside.

たくさんの水みずが長者ちょうじゃの田たんぼに流ながれて入はいってきました。

Takusan no mizu ga chōja no tanbo ni nagarete haitte kimashita.

A great deal of water flowed into the rich man's rice field.

そして水みずは、夜よるの間あいだに田たんぼを水みずの下したに深ふかくかくしてしまったのです。

Soshite mizu wa, yoru no aida ni tanbo o mizu no shita ni fukaku kakushite shimatta no desu.

And during the night, the water completely submerged the rice field deep beneath its surface.

Vocabulary
  • 外そと (soto) — outside
  • 大変たいへん (taihen) — serious; terrible; troubling
  • 起おこる (okoru) — to happen; to occur
  • 水みず (mizu) — water
  • 流ながれる (nagareru) — to flow
  • 入はいる (hairu) — to enter; to come in
  • 間あいだ (aida) — an interval; during a period
  • 下した (shita) — under; beneath
  • 深ふかい (fukai) — deep
  • かくす (kakusu) — to hide; here, to cover from view
Grammar Notes
  • ～間あいだに — This pattern marks a period within which an event takes place.
  • ～てしまう — This pattern emphasizes that an action is completed, often with an unfortunate or irreversible result.
The Field Is Gone14 / 15
次つぎの朝あさ、田たんぼはもうありませんでした。

Tsugi no asa, tanbo wa mō arimasen deshita.

The next morning, the rice field was gone.

そのかわりに、広ひろい広ひろい一ひとつの池いけ、今いまの湖山池こやまいけがありました。

Sono kawari ni, hiroi hiroi hitotsu no ike, ima no Koyamaike ga arimashita.

In its place was one very, very large pond: today's Koyama Pond.

長者ちょうじゃの田たんぼは全部ぜんぶ、なくなってしまいました。

Chōja no tanbo wa zenbu, nakunatte shimaimashita.

The rich man's entire rice field had disappeared.

Vocabulary
  • かわり (kawari) — replacement; in place of something
  • 一ひとつ (hitotsu) — one thing; one, using the general counter
  • なくなる (nakunaru) — to disappear; to cease to exist
The Villagers' Explanation15 / 15
どうして水みずが出でてきて長者ちょうじゃの田たんぼを水みずの下したにかくしてしまったのでしょうか。

Dōshite mizu ga dete kite chōja no tanbo o mizu no shita ni kakushite shimatta no deshō ka.

Why did the water appear and completely submerge the rich man's rice field?

村むらの人ひとは、「長者ちょうじゃがお日ひさまをもどしたので、神様かみさまが怒おこったからだろう」と言いいました。

Mura no hito wa, "Chōja ga ohisama o modoshita node, kamisama ga okotta kara darō" to iimashita.

The villagers said, "It was probably because the deity became angry when the rich man brought the sun back."

鳥取県とっとりけんの湖山池こやまいけ

Tottori-ken no Koyamaike

Koyama Pond in Tottori Prefecture

Vocabulary
  • 神様かみさま (kamisama) — a deity; a god, referred to respectfully
  • 怒おこる (okoru) — to become angry
Grammar Notes
  • ～からだ — This pattern presents the preceding clause as the reason for an outcome. Here the final assertion becomes tentative.
  • ～だろう — This pattern expresses a conjecture or probable explanation rather than a certain fact.
