Story summary

A blind musician follows a mysterious samurai and is asked to play the tale of a tragic battle. One night, a young woman carrying a baby asks a brave warrior for help.

今いまから八百年以上はっぴゃくねんいじょうも前まえのことである。

Ima kara happyaku-nen ijō mo mae no koto de aru.

This happened more than eight hundred years ago.

その頃ころ、日本にほんには、源氏げんじと平家へいけという二ふたつの大おおきな武士ぶしの家いえがあった。

Sono koro, Nihon ni wa, Genji to Heike to iu futatsu no ōkina bushi no ie ga atta.

At that time, Japan had two great warrior families called the Genji and the Heike.

この二ふたつの家いえの間あいだで戦争せんそうが始はじまった。

Kono futatsu no ie no aida de sensō ga hajimatta.

A war began between these two families.

初はじめは平家へいけが強つよかったが、だんだん源氏げんじが強つよくなった。

Hajime wa Heike ga tsuyokatta ga, dandan Genji ga tsuyoku natta.

At first the Heike were stronger, but gradually the Genji grew stronger.

京都きょうとにいた平家へいけは、壇だんノ浦うら（今いまの山口県下関市やまぐちけんしものせきし）というところまで逃にげた。

Kyōto ni ita Heike wa, Dan-no-ura (ima no Yamaguchi-ken Shimonoseki-shi) to iu tokoro made nigeta.

The Heike, who had been in Kyoto, fled as far as a place called Dan-no-ura, in what is now Shimonoseki City, Yamaguchi Prefecture.

最後さいごに壇だんノ浦うらの海うみの上うえで戦たたかって、とうとう平家へいけが負まけた。

Saigo ni Dan-no-ura no umi no ue de tatakatte, tōtō Heike ga maketa.

Finally they fought on the sea at Dan-no-ura, and at last the Heike lost.

平家へいけの人々ひとびとは、女おんなも小ちいさな子こどもも海うみに飛とび込こんで、ほとんど死しんでしまった。

Heike no hitobito wa, onna mo chiisana kodomo mo umi ni tobikonde, hotondo shinde shimatta.

The people of the Heike, including the women and small children, jumped into the sea, and almost all of them died.

Vocabulary
  • 源氏げんじ (Genji) — the Genji, or Minamoto warrior clan
  • 平家へいけ (Heike) — the Heike, or Taira warrior clan
  • 武士ぶし (bushi) — warrior; samurai
  • 戦争せんそう (sensō) — war
  • 壇だんノ浦うら (Dan-no-ura) — Dan-no-ura, the site of the Heike's final sea battle
  • 逃にげる (nigeru) — to flee; to escape
  • 戦たたかう (tatakau) — to fight; to do battle
  • 負まける (makeru) — to lose; to be defeated
  • 飛とび込こむ (tobikomu) — to jump or plunge into
Grammar Notes
  • NというN — This pattern introduces the name of a person, place, or thing: a noun called or named something.
  • い形容詞けいようしの語幹ごかん＋くなる — This pattern changes an adjective into a description of becoming that way. It expresses a change in strength, frequency, or another quality.
  • Vてしまう — This pattern presents an action as completed, often with regret or an unfortunate result. Here it emphasizes the tragedy of the deaths.

この壇だんノ浦うらの戦たたかいの後あと、死しんだ平家へいけの人々ひとびとが幽霊ゆうれいになって、海うみに出でてくるようになった。

Kono Dan-no-ura no tatakai no ato, shinda Heike no hitobito ga yūrei ni natte, umi ni dete kuru yō ni natta.

After this battle at Dan-no-ura, the dead people of the Heike became ghosts and began appearing out at sea.

幽霊ゆうれいは、泳およいでいる人ひとの足あしを海うみの中なかへ引ひき込こんだり、夜よる、海うみの上うえに鬼火おにびと呼よばれる火ひを出だしたりして、人々ひとびとを驚おどろかせた。

Yūrei wa, oyoide iru hito no ashi o umi no naka e hikikondari, yoru, umi no ue ni onibi to yobareru hi o dashitari shite, hitobito o odorokaseta.

The ghosts frightened people by dragging swimmers down into the sea and making flames called ghost fires appear above the sea at night.

そこで、幽霊ゆうれいが出でないようにと、海うみの近ちかくに阿弥陀寺あみだじという寺てらが建たてられた。

Sokode, yūrei ga denai yō ni to, umi no chikaku ni Amidaji to iu tera ga taterareta.

So a temple called Amidaji was built near the sea in the hope that the ghosts would stop appearing.

寺てらの隣となりには墓はかも建たてられた。

Tera no tonari ni wa haka mo taterareta.

Graves were also built beside the temple.

墓はかの石いしには、海うみで死しんだ平家へいけの人々ひとびとの名前なまえが書かかれた。

Haka no ishi ni wa, umi de shinda Heike no hitobito no namae ga kakareta.

The names of the Heike people who had died at sea were written on the gravestones.

それからは、幽霊ゆうれいが出でることは少すくなくなった。

Sore kara wa, yūrei ga deru koto wa sukunaku natta.

After that, the ghosts appeared less often.

Vocabulary
  • 幽霊ゆうれい (yūrei) — ghost
  • 引ひき込こむ (hikikomu) — to pull or drag into
  • 鬼火おにび (onibi) — ghost fire; a supernatural flame
  • 驚おどろかせる (odorokaseru) — to frighten; to startle someone
  • 阿弥陀寺あみだじ (Amidaji) — Amidaji, the Buddhist temple in the story
  • 墓はか (haka) — grave; tomb
Grammar Notes
  • Vようになる — This pattern describes a change in what happens or what someone can do. Here it means that the ghosts began appearing.
  • VたりVたりする — This pattern lists representative actions without implying that they are the only actions performed.
  • Vないように — This pattern expresses the aim of preventing something from happening. The following quotation marker conveys the intention behind building the temple.
  • Vれる・Vられる — This pattern makes the affected person or thing the focus: something is called, built, written, guided, or cut by someone, often without naming the agent.

さて、それから何百年なんびゃくねんも経たった後あとのことである。

Sate, sore kara nanbyaku-nen mo tatta ato no koto de aru.

Now, this happened hundreds of years later.

この壇だんノ浦うらに、芳一ほういちという男おとこが住すんでいた。

Kono Dan-no-ura ni, Hōichi to iu otoko ga sunde ita.

A man called Hoichi lived here in Dan-no-ura.

芳一ほういちは生うまれたときから目めが見みえなかったので、琵琶法師びわほうしになった。

Hōichi wa umareta toki kara me ga mienakatta node, biwa hōshi ni natta.

Hoichi had been blind since birth, so he became a biwa minstrel.

琵琶びわを弾ひきながら、人々ひとびとにいろいろな話はなしを歌うたって聞きかせるのだ。

Biwa o hiki nagara, hitobito ni iroiro na hanashi o utatte kikaseru no da.

He would sing various stories for people while playing the biwa.

芳一ほういちは琵琶びわがとても上手じょうずで、源氏げんじと平家へいけの戦たたかいの話はなしをするのが得意とくいだった。

Hōichi wa biwa ga totemo jōzu de, Genji to Heike no tatakai no hanashi o suru no ga tokui datta.

Hoichi was very skilled at the biwa and excelled at telling the story of the fighting between the Genji and the Heike.

特とくに、壇だんノ浦うらの激はげしく悲かなしい戦たたかいの話はなしをすると、聞きいている人々ひとびとは、みんな泣ないた。

Toku ni, Dan-no-ura no hageshiku kanashii tatakai no hanashi o suru to, kiite iru hitobito wa, minna naita.

In particular, when he told the story of the fierce and tragic battle at Dan-no-ura, everyone listening cried.

阿弥陀寺あみだじの住職じゅうしょく（寺てらの主人しゅじん）も、芳一ほういちの琵琶びわをすばらしいと思おもっていた。

Amidaji no jūshoku (tera no shujin) mo, Hōichi no biwa o subarashii to omotte ita.

The head priest of Amidaji, the person in charge of the temple, also thought Hoichi's biwa playing was wonderful.

よく芳一ほういちを寺てらに呼よんで琵琶びわを弾ひかせた。

Yoku Hōichi o tera ni yonde biwa o hikaseta.

He often invited Hoichi to the temple and had him play the biwa.

そのうちに、住職じゅうしょくは芳一ほういちに寺てらの中なかの一部屋ひとへやを与あたえた。

Sono uchi ni, jūshoku wa Hōichi ni tera no naka no hitoheya o ataeta.

In time, the head priest gave Hoichi a room in the temple.

そこで、芳一ほういちは寺てらに住すむことになり、用事ようじのない晩ばんはいつも、住職じゅうしょくのために琵琶びわを弾ひいた。

Sokode, Hōichi wa tera ni sumu koto ni nari, yōji no nai ban wa itsumo, jūshoku no tame ni biwa o hiita.

So Hoichi came to live at the temple, and on every evening when he had nothing to do, he played the biwa for the head priest.

ある夏なつの夜よるのことである。

Aru natsu no yoru no koto de aru.

It happened one summer night.

住職じゅうしょくは用事ようじがあって出でかけた。

Jūshoku wa yōji ga atte dekaketa.

The head priest went out on an errand.

寺てらにいるのは芳一ほういちだけだった。

Tera ni iru no wa Hōichi dake datta.

Hoichi was the only person at the temple.

その日ひは、とても暑あつかった。

Sono hi wa, totemo atsukatta.

It was very hot that day.

芳一ほういちは部屋へやを出でて、縁側えんがわに座すわって琵琶びわの練習れんしゅうをしていた。

Hōichi wa heya o dete, engawa ni suwatte biwa no renshū o shite ita.

Hoichi left his room, sat on the veranda, and practiced the biwa.

夜遅よるおそくなっても、住職じゅうしょくは帰かえってこなかった。

Yoru osoku natte mo, jūshoku wa kaette konakatta.

Even when it grew late at night, the head priest did not return.

すると、だれかが裏うらの門もんから入はいってきた。

Suru to, dareka ga ura no mon kara haitte kita.

Then someone came in through the back gate.

その足音あしおとは、庭にわを通とおって芳一ほういちの前まえで止とまった。

Sono ashioto wa, niwa o tōtte Hōichi no mae de tomatta.

The footsteps crossed the garden and stopped in front of Hoichi.

住職じゅうしょくではなかった。

Jūshoku de wa nakatta.

It was not the head priest.

武士ぶしのようだった。

Bushi no yō datta.

It seemed to be a warrior.

その武士ぶしは、「芳一ほういち！」と、大おおきな声こえで名前なまえを呼よんだ。

Sono bushi wa, "Hōichi!" to, ōkina koe de namae o yonda.

The warrior called his name in a loud voice: "Hoichi!"

男おとこの声こえだった。

Otoko no koe datta.

It was a man's voice.

Vocabulary
  • 経たつ (tatsu) — to pass, of time
  • 琵琶法師びわほうし (biwa hōshi) — a traditionally blind minstrel who sings stories while playing the biwa
  • 琵琶びわ (biwa) — biwa, a Japanese plucked lute
  • 弾ひく (hiku) — to play a stringed instrument
  • 得意とくい (tokui) — being especially good at something; one's specialty
  • 住職じゅうしょく (jūshoku) — head priest of a Buddhist temple
  • 与あたえる (ataeru) — to give; to provide
  • 用事ようじ (yōji) — errand; business to attend to
  • 縁側えんがわ (engawa) — wooden veranda along the edge of a traditional building
  • 足音あしおと (ashioto) — sound of footsteps
Grammar Notes
  • Vます語幹ごかん＋ながら — This pattern connects two actions performed by the same person at the same time, meaning while doing something.
  • Vせる・Vさせる — This pattern means to make, let, or have someone do an action. Here it describes singing for people to hear and having Hoichi play.
  • Vことになる — This pattern presents an arrangement or outcome that comes about through circumstances. Here Hoichi comes to live at the temple.
  • Vても — This pattern means even if or even though, indicating that the following situation holds despite the preceding condition.
  • Nのようだ・Vようだ — This pattern expresses an impression or inference, meaning seems to be. Hoichi judges what is around him from what he hears.

Vocabulary
  • 泊とまる (tomaru) — to stay overnight; to lodge
  • ご主人様しゅじんさま (goshujin-sama) — master, respectfully referring to the person one serves
  • しかたなく (shikata naku) — having no choice; reluctantly
  • 立たち止どまる (tachidomaru) — to stop walking; to come to a halt
  • 以外いがい (igai) — other than; except for
Grammar Notes
  • Vてくれ — This pattern is a direct, familiar request or command asking someone to do something for the speaker.
  • Vとおりに — This pattern means in accordance with or exactly as described. Here it means doing as the warrior says.
  • Vなければならない — This pattern expresses obligation, meaning must or have to. Its past form describes an obligation that applied in those days.
  • Vろ・しろ — This pattern gives a blunt command. It is used to order the gate opened and to relay the master's order to perform.
